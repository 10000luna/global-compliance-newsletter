Gulf Region
Saudi Arabia Accelerates Anti-Corruption and Tax Compliance as UAE Enters FATF Mutual Evaluation
This week, the Gulf region's compliance landscape was defined by two converging priorities. In Saudi Arabia, the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) advanced Wave 24 of its mandatory e-invoicing programme, requiring businesses with annual turnover above SAR 375,000 (~EUR 85,000) to integrate clearance and e-reporting obligations by June 30, 2026—a deadline that coincides with the scheduled expiry of the Kingdom's tax penalty waiver initiative, lending the period heightened urgency for corporate compliance teams. Separately, the Nazaha (National Anti-Corruption Authority) continued its high-tempo enforcement programme, consistent with the expanded investigative mandate granted under the 2024 Nazaha Law, while the Capital Market Authority (CMA) affirmed its market integrity commitment following its May 2026 enforcement action imposing SR 18 million in fines against 11 former directors of Middle East Healthcare Company for financial statement manipulation. In the UAE, the country's financial system entered its most consequential external scrutiny in years as the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) commenced its June 2026 mutual evaluation; regulators—including the DFSA and the Central Bank UAE—are maintaining heightened enforcement postures, building on the CBUAE's April 2026 updated AML/CFT/CPF guidance and ongoing whistleblower protection reforms. The dual pressures of domestic reform and international evaluation make the Gulf one of the most compliance-active regions globally this week.
本周，海湾地区合规态势由两大优先议题主导。在沙特阿拉伯，税务和海关总署（ZATCA）推进了电子发票强制项目第24批次，要求年营业额超过37.5万里亚尔（约合8.5万欧元）的企业于2026年6月30日前完成B2B/B2G发票清算及B2C电子申报义务——该截止日与王国税务处罚豁免计划的到期日重合，为企业合规团队增添了额外紧迫性。纳扎哈（国家反腐局）依据2024年《纳扎哈法》扩大的调查授权持续保持高强度执法；资本市场管理局（CMA）则于2026年5月就中东医疗保健公司11名前任董事操纵财务报表一事处以合计1800万里亚尔罚款，重申市场诚信立场。在阿联酋，金融行动特别工作组（FATF）于2026年6月正式启动对阿联酋的相互评估，迪拜金融服务局（DFSA）和阿联酋央行（CBUAE）在央行2026年4月更新AML/CFT/CPF指引及举报人保护改革的基础上，持续保持强化执法态势。国内改革与国际评估的双重压力，使海湾地区成为本周全球合规态势最为活跃的地区之一。

Asia-Pacific
Japan Passes Landmark FIEA Crypto Bill; Indonesia KPK Arrests Deputy Minister in $9M Extortion Scheme; Singapore and Thailand Tighten Data and AML Frameworks
The Asia-Pacific region produced some of the most significant compliance developments globally this week. Most notably, Japan's House of Representatives passed a sweeping bill on June 11, 2026 to reclassify crypto-assets as financial instruments under the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA), introducing insider-trading prohibitions, mandatory issuer disclosures, and maximum prison terms increased from 3 to 10 years for unlicensed operations; the bill now moves to the Upper House where passage is expected. In Indonesia, the Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) arrested Deputy Minister of Immigration Silmy Karim on June 4 as part of a sweep detaining 17 individuals across an alleged residence-permit extortion scheme valued at approximately US$9 million per week—the KPK's seventh OTT operation of 2026. Singapore's PDPC launched a public consultation on proposed advisory guidelines for the use of personal data in generative AI systems (June 2), spanning the full GenAI lifecycle including machine unlearning and upstream data verification; the MAS also imposed a S$300,000 composition penalty on Padang Trust for AML/CFT control failures. In Thailand, the SEC rolled out a major anti-financial crime initiative mandating enhanced KYC, stricter CDD, real-time beneficial ownership reporting, and inter-agency information sharing with AMLO. Hong Kong's HKMA and SFC concluded consultation on OTC derivatives clearing rule amendments (June 5), standardising calculation periods effective March 2027. Malaysia's Prime Minister Anwar reinforced MACC's anti-corruption mandate at a June 11 meeting with the Commission's newly appointed Chief Commissioner.

Europe
BaFin Escalates AML Enforcement Against Nomura's Instinet Germany; PFOF Grandfathering Period Ends 30 June; Hungary NAIH Issues Data Minimisation Ruling
European compliance activity this week centred on Germany. BaFin on June 10, 2026 imposed escalated remedial action on Instinet Germany—a Nomura subsidiary and prominent dark pool operator—after repeated AML deficiencies went unresolved despite a prior remediation plan. Deficiencies spanned risk analysis, money laundering officer appointment, customer onboarding, risk assessment, and enhanced customer due diligence, signalling BaFin's intent to escalate where remediation frameworks are repeatedly deficient. Concurrently, Germany's EU MiFIR transitional exemption allowing payment for order flow (PFOF) with domestic clients expires June 30, 2026—the only such grandfathering arrangement in the EU—requiring German neobrokers and retail platforms to complete compliance reconfiguration before month's end. BaFin has confirmed it will not exempt institutions from post-deadline obligations. In Hungary, the National Authority for Data Protection (NAIH) issued a notable recent decision finding a private university liable for GDPR data minimisation violations, imposing a €5,000 penalty and clarifying that the obligation to enforce data minimisation rests entirely with the data controller—the controller cannot discharge this obligation by allowing individuals to self-limit their submissions.

Data Protection
PDPC Launches Consultation on Proposed Guidelines for Use of Personal Data in Generative AI  个人数据保护委员会就生成式AI中个人数据使用指南启动公众咨询
On 2 June 2026, Singapore's Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) published proposed advisory guidelines on the use of personal data in generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) systems for public consultation. The guidelines span three stages of the GenAI lifecycle—data collection and training, model development, and deployment—and address risks including the unauthorised entry of personal data into training datasets, confidentiality obligations on organisations deploying third-party GenAI products, and technical measures such as machine unlearning to remove inaccurate or consent-withdrawn personal data from models. This represents a significant extension of the PDPC's March 2024 AI advisory guidelines (which covered AI recommendation and decision systems) to the distinct privacy challenges of generative models. Public comments are invited.
2026年6月2日，新加坡个人数据保护委员会（PDPC）就生成式人工智能（GenAI）系统中个人数据使用的拟议指导方针发布公众咨询文件，涵盖GenAI生命周期三个阶段：数据收集与训练、模型开发及部署。指南针对的核心风险包括：个人数据在未经授权情况下进入训练数据集、部署第三方GenAI产品机构的保密义务，以及采用"机器遗忘"等技术手段从模型中删除不准确或已撤销同意个人数据等。本次咨询是PDPC继2024年3月AI推荐与决策系统指引后的重大扩展，专门针对生成式模型的独特隐私挑战。公众意见征集现已开放。

Thailand SEC Launches Five-Measure Anti-Financial Crime Initiative for Capital Markets  泰国证监会推出资本市场五项反金融犯罪举措
Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission launched a major anti-financial crime initiative for capital market intermediaries, comprising five integrated measures designed to detect and disrupt illicit fund flows. The initiative requires securities firms to implement tighter know-your-customer (KYC) protocols—including enhanced verification of client income and occupation—alongside stricter customer due diligence and real-time transaction monitoring. Listed companies face mandatory disclosure requirements: detailed beneficial ownership structures and shareholding changes must be reported within three business days (T+3). Unusual or suspicious transactions must trigger enhanced CDD and formal Suspicious Activity Reports to AMLO. The initiative was developed through coordination between the SEC, AMLO, the Central Investigation Bureau, and industry participants, as part of the SEC's 2026–2028 strategic plan.

Japan's Lower House Passes FIEA Amendment Reclassifying Crypto-Assets as Financial Instruments  日本众议院通过修正案，将加密资产重新归类为金融工具
On 11 June 2026, Japan's House of Representatives passed a bill amending the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA) to reclassify crypto-assets as financial instruments, moving them out of the Payment Services Act and into the same regulatory framework as stocks and bonds. The legislation introduces insider-trading prohibitions (extending securities-law enforcement to digital asset markets), mandatory annual issuer disclosures, harsher penalties (maximum prison terms for unlicensed operations raised from 3 to 10 years; fines from ¥3M to ¥10M), and a pathway for crypto ETFs.
